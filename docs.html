Global 30×30 work in progress

An interactive map and AI assistant for exploring the spatial and social context of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework's "30×30" target — the commitment that at least 30% of the planet's land and waters be effectively conserved by 2030.

← Open the App

About this project

A project of the Eric and Wendy Schmidt Center for Data Science & Environment (DSE) at UC Berkeley.

In 2022, nearly 200 nations adopted the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework. Its Target 3 — the "30×30" goal — commits the world to effectively conserving at least 30% of terrestrial, inland-water, marine and coastal areas by 2030. Meeting it depends on knowing what is already protected, where biodiversity and irreplaceable carbon are concentrated, and how the network overlaps with the Indigenous peoples and local communities who steward much of the planet's remaining biodiversity. This app brings the global protected- and conserved-area inventory together with the biodiversity, carbon, land-cover, freshwater, and population datasets that inform those questions, and pairs them with an AI assistant that can both drive the map and answer quantitative questions about the data.

The app is inspired by the analysis in Fajardo et al. 2026, Social implications of the 30×30 global conservation target (Nature Communications). Not every layer from that study is hosted here — see the repository's coverage notes for what can and cannot currently be reproduced.

This is a work in progress — layers, models, and capabilities are being added and refined.

How it works

This app is powered by GLEN, DSE's open-source framework for AI-assisted geospatial exploration. Open datasets stream straight to your browser from cloud-native formats — no tile server in between — and an AI assistant answers questions by writing live queries against the same data. Full technical details are in the GLEN documentation.

🌱 Low-carbon, open models. Be patient. While much faster than most humans, GLEN makes use of more environmentally-friendly AI models. (Check out the carbon dashboard 🌱 to see the CO₂/energy use of this deployment.)

Exploring the map

The map is built on MapLibre GL — the open-source engine behind the familiar Mapbox-style interactive maps. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan, and hold Ctrl and drag to tilt and rotate the view. Ask the assistant to fly to any country, region, or place by name and it will look up the location and recenter the map for you.

The Overlays panel lists every data layer, organized into groups — protected & conserved areas, Indigenous & community lands, biodiversity, carbon, freshwater, and more. Toggle a layer on or off with its checkbox, and hover any feature for a tooltip of its live attributes — names, designations, IUCN categories, areas — straight from the source data. When layers overlap, use the send-to-back button (↩) in the Overlays header to drop the topmost layer beneath the others.

Asking the assistant

The Data Assistant understands plain English and does two kinds of things: it can drive the map for you, and it can answer analytical questions about the data. Just type what you want — if it can't do something, it will say so and usually suggest the closest thing it can.

Map operations happen right away on the layers already loaded — no hunting through menus:

  • Filter a layer: "Show only IUCN category I and II protected areas."
  • Recolor or restyle a layer: "Color protected areas by designation type", "Make the ecoregion layer 50% transparent."
  • Fly somewhere: "Fly to the Amazon", "Zoom to the Congo Basin."
  • Show or hide layers: "Turn on irrecoverable carbon", "Show Indigenous and community lands."

Analytical questions are answered by writing live spatial queries over the underlying data, returning a number or a downloadable table:

  • "What percentage of global land is currently inside a protected area or OECM?"
  • "How many people live inside existing protected areas?"
  • "Which ecoregions are least represented in the current protected-area network?"
✏️ Draw an area to ask about it. Use the draw tool on the map to sketch a polygon — a basin, a country, a proposed corridor, anywhere on Earth — then ask the assistant about it: "How much of the area I drew is already conserved?", "What's the average irrecoverable carbon here?", "How many people live inside this polygon?" Your drawn shape becomes a live spatial filter for any query.

Data layers & sources

Every layer draws from a public, primary source. Click "STAC" beside any entry to see the full machine-readable metadata — schemas, column descriptions, license, lineage, and download URLs.

Protected & Conserved Areas

Protected Areas (WDPA)
The World Database on Protected Areas — the global inventory of legally designated and proposed protected areas, attributed with designation, IUCN management category, status, country, and area. The primary reference for tracking 30×30 progress.
STACSource: Protected Planet
UNEP-WCMC & IUCN — WDPA
OECMs (WD-OECM)
The World Database on Other Effective Area-Based Conservation Measures — the sister database to WDPA. Target 3 is met by the union of WDPA and WD-OECM, so both are needed for any "currently conserved" figure.
STACSource: Protected Planet
UNEP-WCMC & IUCN — WD-OECM

Indigenous & Community Lands

Indigenous & Community Lands (LandMark)
Community-mapped Indigenous and local-community lands compiled by the LandMark global platform — context for the lands and territories that overlap with, and steward, much of the world's remaining biodiversity.
STACSource: LandMark
LandMark — Global Platform of Indigenous and Community Lands
ICCA Registry (Polygon & Point Sites)
Self-reported Indigenous and Community Conserved Areas curated by UNEP-WCMC and the ICCA Consortium — split into mapped-boundary (polygon) and location-only (point) sites. Submission is opt-in by communities, so this is an illustrative registry, not a complete inventory.
STAC (polygon)STAC (point)Source: ICCA Registry
UNEP-WCMC & the ICCA Consortium

Biodiversity Context

Terrestrial Ecoregions (WWF)
WWF's 846 terrestrial ecoregions across 14 biomes and 8 realms, attributed with Nature Needs Half protection status — the standard framework for assessing how representative the protected-area network is across the planet's habitats.
STACSource: WWF
Olson et al. 2001 / Dinerstein et al. 2017
Species ranges, occurrences & status (assistant only)
IUCN Red List range maps and taxonomy (2025), GBIF occurrences, and iNaturalist modeled ranges are queryable through the assistant rather than pre-loaded as map toggles. Ask for richness, threatened-species counts, or representation and the assistant computes them on demand from the H3-indexed source data.
Source: IUCN Red ListSource: GBIFSource: iNaturalist
IUCN 2025 · GBIF · iNaturalist

Nature's Contributions to People

Biodiversity & Natural Habitat (Chaplin-Kramer 2019)
A 300 m composite index of biodiversity and remaining natural habitat from the global modeling of nature's contributions to people — used to frame the biodiversity value of conserved vs. unconserved land.
STACSource: Chaplin-Kramer et al. 2019
Chaplin-Kramer et al. 2019, Science
Irrecoverable & Vulnerable Carbon (2024)
Manageable ecosystem carbon that, if lost, could not be recovered by 2050 (irrecoverable) or is at risk from land-use conversion (vulnerable), at 300 m — a key argument for protecting specific places, not just total area.
STACSource: Irrecoverable Carbon
Noon et al. 2022 / Conservation International

Population

Population Density (GHS-POP 2020)
The Global Human Settlement Layer's 90 m gridded population count for 2020 — the basis for "how many people live inside or near protected areas" analyses.
STACSource: GHSL
European Commission JRC — GHS-POP

Ecosystems & Freshwater

Land Cover (Copernicus 100 m, 2019)
Copernicus Global Land Cover at 100 m, FAO LCCS classes — global land-cover context that also serves as a land mask for distinguishing terrestrial from marine coverage.
STACSource: Copernicus
Copernicus Global Land Service — CGLS-LC100
Ramsar Wetlands (Intl. Importance)
Wetlands of international importance designated under the Ramsar Convention — a recognized conservation designation complementing WDPA and OECMs.
STACSource: Ramsar
Ramsar Convention / Ramsar Sites Information Service
Watersheds (HydroBASINS L3)
HydroSHEDS HydroBASINS global watershed boundaries (level 3 shown as a layer; finer levels available to the assistant) — the freshwater analysis unit for basin-scale summaries.
STACSource: HydroSHEDS
Lehner & Grill 2013 — HydroBASINS v1c

Administrative Boundaries

Countries & Regions / States
Overture Maps administrative divisions — country and region/state boundaries for jurisdiction-level breakdowns and reference framing.
STAC (countries)STAC (regions)Source: Overture Maps
Overture Maps Foundation
